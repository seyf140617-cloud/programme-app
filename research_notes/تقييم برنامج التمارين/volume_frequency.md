## Q1. Dose-response of weekly sets for hypertrophy, diminishing returns, and how to count direct vs. indirect sets

### Takeaway
The strongest current evidence is Pelland et al. (Sports Medicine, 2025 online / 2026 print). Its meta-regression of 67 studies found hypertrophy keeps rising with weekly sets, with diminishing returns and no clear plateau in the range studied. The returns are steepest up to about 10 sets. Indirect (synergist) sets fit best when each counts as 0.5 of a set ("fractional" counting). Older work (Schoenfeld 2017: 10+ sets beats fewer; Baz-Valle 2022: 12-20 sets per week as a practical target) agrees with this. Few studies have tested more than about 25 fractional sets per week, so the curve above that point is uncertain.

### Cited Findings
**Pelland, Remmert, Robinson, Hinson & Zourdos — "The Resistance Training Dose Response: Meta-Regressions Exploring the Effects of Weekly Volume and Frequency on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gains", Sports Medicine (accepted 14 Oct 2025; PubMed 41343037; a secondary source cites it as 2026;56(2):481–505). Preprint: SportRxiv 2024, doi 10.51224/SRXIV.460.**
- Design: multilevel Bayesian meta-regressions covering 67 studies and 2,058 participants, adjusted for intervention length and training status. Every set was classified as direct or indirect, and indirect sets were counted three ways: 1 ("total"), 0.5 ("fractional") or 0 ("direct"). — [SportRxiv preprint page](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/)
- "The relative evidence for the 'fractional' quantification method was strongest." The volume slope had a 100% posterior probability of being above zero for hypertrophy, but the best-fit models showed diminishing returns, and these were "considerably more pronounced" for strength. — [SportRxiv preprint page](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460)
- Marginal slope: +0.24% muscle size per extra weekly fractional set (95% CrI 0.15-0.33) at the average volume of 12.25 fractional sets. Schoenfeld et al. 2017 had estimated +0.38% per set. — [Published PDF (fisiologiadelejercicio.com copy)](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf)
- Predicted muscle size gain by weekly fractional sets (Fig. 7A): 10 sets ≈ 4.2%, 20 ≈ 6.8%, 30 ≈ 8.5%, 40 ≈ 10.1%. — [Published PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf)
- Hypertrophy "efficiency tiers" (Table 3), where the smallest detectable effect is 2.05%:
  - Minimum effective dose = 4 fractional sets per week.
  - "Higher efficiency" = 5-10 sets; about 6 more sets are needed for each further detectable gain.
  - "Intermediate" = 11-18 sets; about 8.5 more sets per detectable gain.
  - "Lower" = 19-29 sets; about 10.75 more sets per detectable gain.
  - "Lowest" = 30-42 sets; about 12.5 more sets per detectable gain.
  - 43+ sets = "Unclear… insufficient data or potentially less hypertrophy."
  — [Published PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf)
- Caveats from the authors: "caution is warranted as few studies have explored ~25+ 'fractional' weekly sets"; the best-fit model "is still compatible with multiple functional forms (e.g., functional plateau, inverted-U)." The data favoured diminishing returns, not an inverted U. — [Published PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf)
- Direct vs. indirect definitions (hypertrophy): a set is direct when the measured muscle is "likely to be the primary force generator," and indirect when it is "meaningfully trained but not the primary force generator (synergist)." Worked example: 5 curls + 5 rows = 7.5 fractional biceps sets. Table 1 lists indirect exercises:
  - Biceps: lat pulldown, neutral-grip pulldown, seated row, machine rows, bent-over row.
  - Triceps: flat, incline and decline bench, shoulder press, machine press.
  - Trapezius: pulldown and seated row only (no direct exercise listed).
  — [Published PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf)
- Mean volume across the included hypertrophy effects was about 8 sets per muscle per week, and the sample was young (mean age about 25) and about 79% male. This is a secondary summary and was not checked against the paper's tables. — [Root Strength summary](https://www.rootstrengthseattle.com/blog/training-volume-dose-response)

**Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2017 — "Dose-response relationship between weekly resistance training volume and increases in muscle mass", J Sports Sci 35(11):1073-82.**
- Data: 15 studies, 34 treatment groups. Weekly sets as a continuous variable had a significant effect (P = 0.002).
- Categorical results: <5 sets = 5.4% gain, 5-9 sets = 6.6%, 10+ sets = 9.8%.
- Sparse data meant the dose-response could only be shown up to about 10 or more weekly sets per muscle.
— [ResearchGate PDF](https://researchgate.net/profile/Brad-Schoenfeld/publication/305455324_Dose-response_relationship_between_weekly_resistance_training_volume_and_increases_in_muscle_mass_A_systematic_review_and_meta-analysis/links/59dc0269458515e9ab4527d6/Dose-response-relationship-between-weekly-resistance-training-volume-and-increases-in-muscle-mass-A-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis.pdf)

**Baz-Valle, Balsalobre-Fernández, Alix-Fages & Santos-Concejero 2022 — "A Systematic Review of the Effects of Different Resistance Training Volumes on Muscle Hypertrophy", J Hum Kinet 81:199-210.**
- Data: 7 studies (6 meta-analysed), young trained men aged 18-35. Volume bands: low <12, moderate 12-20, high >20 sets per week.
- Moderate and high volume gave similar results for quadriceps and biceps. High volume was better for triceps (p = 0.01).
- Conclusion: 12-20 weekly sets per muscle "may be an optimum standard recommendation."
— [PMC8884877](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8884877)

**Other studies and position stands**
- Schoenfeld et al. 2019, Med Sci Sports Exerc 51(1):94-103 (trained men): 1, 3 or 5 sets per exercise. The 5-set group did up to 45 sets per week for the upper and lower limbs, and hypertrophy increased with volume while strength did not. — [PubMed 30153194](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30153194/); [PMC6303131](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6303131/)
- Counter-view: Buckner et al. 2023 (J Trainology 12(2):29-36), "There are still doubts", argued the volume-hypertrophy dose-response is not settled. Krieger published a rebuttal. — cited in the [Pelland preprint reference list](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/460) and summarised in a [search-result summary of Biolayne coverage](https://biolayne.com/reps/issue-24/volume-and-hypertrophy-doubts-about-the-doubts/) (page not fetchable)
- ACSM 2026 Position Stand (Currier et al.; overview of reviews): "Muscle hypertrophy was enhanced by higher volumes (≥10 sets/wk)" with a dose-response. ACSM's press summary says hypertrophy needs "higher weekly volume (~10 sets per muscle group)." — [PubMed 41843416](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/); [PMC12965823](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/); [ACSM news](https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/)

### Inferences
- An evidence-based weekly target for a recreational lifter is about 10-20 fractional sets per muscle (Pelland's "higher" and "intermediate" tiers, Baz-Valle's 12-20, ACSM's 10 or more). Above about 20 sets, each detectable gain costs about 11-12 more sets, so that range suits only priority muscles and only if recovery allows.
- Use fractional counting: direct sets count 1, synergist sets count 0.5. On that basis the program's biceps and triceps get much more total stimulus than their direct counts suggest.

### Gaps
- Pelland's final journal page numbers (56(2):481-505) come from a secondary blog. The publisher PDF copy confirms Sports Medicine and acceptance on 14 Oct 2025 but shows no volume or pages.
- Pelland's data do not allow per-muscle curves (e.g., calves vs. quads). No muscle-specific dose-response was found beyond Baz-Valle's quadriceps, biceps and triceps results.
- The NSCA and ISSN position stands were not retrieved. The IUSCA 2021 position stand (Schoenfeld et al., Int J Strength Cond) exists, but its numbers were not checked.

## Q2. Training frequency per muscle per week when volume is equated

### Takeaway
At equal weekly volume, frequency has little or no independent effect on hypertrophy. That holds across the meta-analyses (Schoenfeld 2019, Pelland 2025/26, ACSM 2026, a 2026 medRxiv analysis). Frequency is mainly a tool for spreading volume so that each session stays productive. Training each muscle twice a week, as this program does, is well supported and needs no change.

### Cited Findings
- Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2016 (Sports Med 46(11):1689-97): at equal volume, training a muscle twice a week "promote[s] superior hypertrophic outcomes to once a week." — [PubMed 27102172](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27102172/)
- Schoenfeld, Grgic & Krieger 2019 (J Sports Sci 37(11):1286-95): "no significant difference between higher and lower frequency on a volume-equated basis… strong evidence that resistance training frequency does not significantly or meaningfully impact muscle hypertrophy when volume is equated." — [PubMed 30558493](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30558493/)
- A 2026 medRxiv re-synthesis summarised the two Schoenfeld meta-analyses:
  - 2016: 7 comparisons, ES 0.19 (95% CI 0.11-0.28), mostly untrained participants and indirect measures.
  - 2019: 13 volume-equated studies, ES 0.07 (95% CI -0.02 to 0.17). Restricted to direct measures, 0.07 (95% CI -0.08 to 0.21; P = 0.32), with a lower-body subgroup of 0.15 (P = 0.08).
  — [medRxiv 2026.10.07.26365015](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.07.26365015v3.full)
- The same medRxiv preprint (not peer reviewed) pooled 16 volume-equated trials of 1-3 sessions per muscle per week: g = -0.02 (95% CI -0.21 to 0.16; I² = 0%). "The data give no basis for preferring one, two or three sessions at a fixed weekly volume." — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.07.26365015v3.full)
- Pelland et al., frequency and hypertrophy: marginal slope +0.32% per session (95% CrI -0.14 to 0.82), with a 91.3% probability of being above zero. This is "compatible with negligible effects." A direct-comparison analysis (15 studies, 78 effects, 370 participants) agreed. Strength did rise with frequency (+3.27% per session, 95% CrI 2.74-3.84). — [Published PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf)
- ACSM 2026 Position Stand: frequency is marked "✖" (no consistent effect) for hypertrophy and "✔" for strength. — [PMC12965823](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/)
- RP's own practitioner guidance for chest: "2-4 times per week works best for most people." — [RP Chest Guide (Israetel)](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/chest-hypertrophy-training-tips)

### Inferences
- Every upper-body muscle in the program is trained twice a week (Mon and Fri), and every lower-body muscle twice (Wed and Sat). Abs are trained directly only once (Sat), and traps directly once (Mon shrugs), with carries on Wed. Frequency itself does not need fixing.
- The main practical value of frequency here is per-session capacity: adding volume to a weak muscle is easier as a third weekly exposure, or as a few sets on another day, than by lengthening the already long upper days.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence was found on how the Tue/Thu swim, sauna and cold-tub sessions affect this lifter's recovery or volume tolerance. That falls outside this scope; another researcher covers recovery.

- The user's training age is unknown, and it strongly affects which tier is appropriate.

## Q4. Are RP's MEV/MAV/MRV landmarks evidence-based or practitioner heuristics?

### Takeaway
They are practitioner heuristics. RP itself says they are averages from coaching experience, with large differences between individuals. The concepts fit the research (a minimum effective dose plus diminishing returns), but the per-muscle numbers have not been tested in trials. The app's figures also do not match RP's own published chest figures, because the app splits chest into two muscles and gives each its own landmarks.

### Cited Findings
- RP's chest guide (Dr. Mike Israetel) gives chest MV 2-4, MEV 4-6, MAV 6-16 and MRV 16-24 sets per week, and says: "Please note that these are averages based on our experience working with lots of clients and our own training," and "it's possible you're significantly higher or lower than these numbers… only through direct experimentation on yourself can you tell." — [RP Chest Guide](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/chest-hypertrophy-training-tips)
- RP's landmark article describes starting a mesocycle at MEV and adding sets weekly toward MRV (e.g., 12 → 14 → 16 → 18 → 20 sets per week). — [RP Volume Landmarks](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/training-volume-landmarks-muscle-growth)
- Third-party summaries call the landmarks "ranges you calibrate to yourself through experimentation." — [Arvo RP guide](https://arvo.guru/resources/methods/rp-training)
- Research comparison: Pelland's minimum effective dose (about 4 fractional sets) is close to RP's typical MEV range (4-6 for chest). However, the research shows no recoverable-volume ceiling (no plateau or inverted U) within the range studied. RP's MRV is therefore a practitioner concept about fatigue and recovery, not a value estimated from trials. — [Published PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf)

### Inferences
- The app's chest landmarks (upper 8/14/22 plus lower 4/8/16) add up to 12/22/38 for the whole pec. RP's single chest guide says 4-6/6-16/16-24. The app's MEV for chest alone is therefore about double RP's, and its combined MRV (38) is far above RP's. The app splits back the same way (lats 10/16/24 plus mid-back 6/12/20). The app's landmarks should be described as "adapted from RP, unvalidated," not as research values.
- Use the landmarks as rough starting points, then adjust from the lifter's own progress and recovery. RP itself frames them this way.

### Gaps
- RP's own guides for back, delts, arms, legs, calves and abs were not retrieved in this pass, so the app's other muscle numbers could not be checked line by line against RP.

## Q5. How many sets per muscle per session before returns diminish?

### Takeaway
A 2025 SportRxiv meta-regression (Remmert, Pelland et al.) found hypertrophy rises with per-session volume, with diminishing returns. The point beyond which no further benefit was detectable was about 11 fractional sets per muscle per session, and about 2 direct sets per exercise for strength. The authors say this is not a hard ceiling. In this program every muscle stays at or below about 10 fractional sets per session, so per-session volume does not limit it.

### Cited Findings
- Remmert, Pelland, Robinson, Hinson & Zourdos, "Is There Too Much of a Good Thing? Meta-Regressions of the Effect of Per-Session Volume on Hypertrophy and Strength", SportRxiv 2 Apr 2025, doi 10.51224/SRXIV.537 (preprint, not peer reviewed):
  - The posterior probability of a positive per-session slope was 100% for both hypertrophy and strength.
  - "Both best fit models suggest diminishing returns… with the PUOS occurring at ~2 'direct' sets for strength and ~11 'fractional' sets for hypertrophy."
  — [SportRxiv 537](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537)
- A 2026 medRxiv paper summarised the same preprint as showing "no detectable advantage beyond about 11 fractional sets per session; its authors caution that this value is not an upper limit beyond which further gains cannot occur." — [medRxiv 2026.10.07.26365015](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.07.26365015v3.full)
- The published Pelland paper's discussion also notes "there is no plateau in hypertrophy with increasing per session volumes" (excerpt in search index). — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398333408_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_Response_Meta-Regressions_Exploring_the_Effects_of_Weekly_Volume_and_Frequency_on_Muscle_Hypertrophy_and_Strength_Gains_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_ResponseJ_C_Pelland_et)
- RP's practitioner view: "In most studies, within 3-8 sets per session, more volume leads to more gains." — [RP article](https://rpstrength.com/blogs/articles/in-defense-of-set-increases-within-the-hypertrophy-mesocycle)

### Inferences
Fractional per-session counts for this program:

| Muscle | Mon | Fri | Wed | Sat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Back (direct pulling sets) | 9 | 10 | – | – |
| Chest | 7 | 7 (with landmine) | – | – |
| Biceps | 2 + 4.5 = 6.5 | 2 + 5 = 7 | – | – |
| Triceps | 3 + 3.5 = 6.5 | 2 + 3.5 = 5.5 | – | – |
| Quads | – | – | 9 (+3 TKE) | 3 (+3 TKE) |
| Hamstrings | – | – | 3 | 6 |

- All of these are at or below about 11 fractional sets per session, so the arms can take 1-2 more direct sets per session without passing the per-session point of diminishing returns.
- Doing all 6 lat-pulldown sets in a row (Mon) and all 6 chest-supported row sets (Fri) is within the per-session limit. Later sets in such a long block are probably done under more fatigue, though no source here measured that directly. Splitting each block across two exercises, or trimming it to 4-5 sets, is a reasonable choice, but optional.

### Gaps
- The Remmert per-session preprint's study count and the width of its estimate's uncertainty interval could not be retrieved, because the PDF host was blocked. Until it is peer reviewed, treat the about 11-set figure as provisional.
