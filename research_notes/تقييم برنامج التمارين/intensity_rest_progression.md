# Intensity (RIR), Rep Ranges, Rest, Supersets, Progression and Deloads: Evidence vs. the User's 4-Day Upper/Lower Program

Context: recreational lifter, ~73 kg, goal hypertrophy. Program (from program.txt): heavy compounds 6–8 or 8–12 reps @ RIR 2, rest 150–180 s; secondary compounds 8–15 reps @ RIR 2, rest 90–120 s; isolation 10–20 reps mostly @ RIR 1 (some RIR 2), rest 60–75 s. Supersets: Mon C1/C2 Face Pull + Barbell Shrug; Wed D1/D2 Single-Leg Extension + Leg Curl, E1/E2 Standing Calf Raise + Farmer's Carry; Fri A1/A2 Hammer Curl + Triceps Pushdown; Sat F1/F2 Pallof Press + Wrist/Reverse Curl. The app uses double progression (top of range at target RIR on all sets → +2.5 kg barbell/machine, +2 kg dumbbell, +1 kg light isolation → reps reset to bottom) and a composite deload score (1RM decline + psych score + volume).

## Q1. Proximity to failure: is RIR 1–2 appropriate, and are RIR self-estimates accurate?

### Takeaway
The evidence supports the program's RIR 1–2 targets: hypertrophy tends to improve as sets end closer to failure, but going all the way to failure adds little or nothing measurable over 1–2 RIR and costs more fatigue. The main weakness is in estimating RIR. Lifters underestimate how many reps they have left by about 1 rep on average, and they are less accurate at high rep counts (>12). So the "RIR 1" target on 12–20-rep isolation work (lateral raises, calves, abduction) may in practice be RIR 2–3.

### Cited Findings
- Refalo et al. 2023 (Sports Medicine 53:649–665; 15 studies): a trivial advantage for set failure vs. non-failure using any definition of failure (ES = 0.19, 95% CI 0.00–0.37, p = 0.045). There was no advantage for momentary muscular failure specifically vs. non-failure (ES = 0.12, 95% CI −0.13 to 0.37, p = 0.343). Higher (>25%) vs. moderate (20–25%) velocity loss did not differ (ES = 0.08). The authors propose a *non-linear* relationship between proximity to failure and hypertrophy. — [PubMed 36334240](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/)
- Robinson et al. 2024 (Sports Medicine 54(9):2209–2231; exploratory multilevel meta-regressions, with RIR estimated from study descriptions): for hypertrophy, the marginal slopes for estimated RIR were negative in every best-fit model and their CIs excluded zero, meaning muscle size gains increased as sets ended closer to failure. For strength, slopes were null across a wide RIR range. The authors say the exact RIR–hypertrophy relationship "remains unclear" and that the analysis is exploratory. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2); [Abertay repository record](https://rke.abertay.ac.uk/en/publications/exploring-the-dose-response-relationship-between-estimated-resist/)
- Refalo, Helms, Robinson, Hamilton & Fyfe 2024 (Journal of Sports Sciences; within-subject trial, 18 resistance-trained people: 12 male, 6 female; 8 weeks, 2 sessions/week, quadriceps): quadriceps thickness gains were essentially identical for failure, 0.181 cm (HDI 0.119–0.243), and 1–2 RIR, 0.182 cm (HDI 0.115–0.247). Velocity loss and rep loss were consistently greater with failure. The plain-language summary says 1–2 RIR "can be sufficient to promote similar hypertrophy… as reaching momentary muscular failure." — [PubMed 38393985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38393985/); [AUT repository](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/dbe641c7-a606-4e00-94bc-bb2fbe253afa)
  - Note: a secondary blog (getfitcraft) describes this trial as "26 adults". The PubMed abstract says n = 18 (12 M, 6 F). Use n = 18.
- Perceptual follow-up from the same trial (European Journal of Sport Science, "The Effect of Proximity-To-Failure on Perceptual Responses to Resistance Training"): session-RPE was 1.1 points higher (HDI 0.6–1.6) with failure than with 1–2 RIR. Discomfort (RPD) was also higher and did not habituate over 8 weeks. The authors suggest 1–2 RIR may help adherence. — [Wiley EJSC](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ejsc.12266)
- Grgic, Schoenfeld, Orazem & Sabol 2022 (Journal of Sport and Health Science 11(2):202–211; 15 studies): no significant difference between failure and non-failure for hypertrophy (ES = 0.22, 95% CI −0.11 to 0.55) or strength (ES = −0.09). In the resistance-trained subgroup, failure had a small significant edge for hypertrophy (ES = 0.15, 95% CI 0.03–0.26). When volume was not equated, non-failure was favoured for strength (ES = −0.32). — [PubMed 33497853](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33497853/)
- A 2026 meta-analysis (BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation, "Effects of resistance training performed to repetition non-failure on exercise performance in healthy adults") found a trivial, non-significant pooled hypertrophy effect for non-failure vs. failure (ES = −0.15, 95% CI −0.39 to 0.09, p = 0.220, I² = 0%). — [Springer/BMC](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-026-01861-z)
- RIR accuracy, Halperin et al. 2022 (Sports Medicine 52:377–390; 12 studies, 414 participants, 262 effects): participants **underpredicted** reps to failure by 0.95 reps on average (95% CI 0.17–1.73), with very high heterogeneity (I² = 97.9%). Accuracy improved when predictions were made closer to failure, in later sets, and with heavier loads or fewer reps (≤12 reps: β = 0.06; >12 reps: β = 0.47). Training status did not affect accuracy. Between-participant SD was 1.45 reps. — [PubMed 34542869](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34542869/); [ResearchGate preprint](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/351730507_Accuracy_in_predicting_repetitions_to_task_failure_in_resistance_exercise_a_scoping_review_and_exploratory_meta-_analysis)

### Inferences
- RIR 2 on heavy compounds (incline barbell press, RDL, box squat, weighted pull-ups, pulldown) is well supported. Robinson 2024 shows strength is insensitive to RIR, and Refalo 2024 shows 1–2 RIR matches failure for hypertrophy with less fatigue and velocity loss. Pushing heavy compounds closer to failure is not warranted, especially RDL and squat, which carry higher technical and fatigue risk.
- RIR 1 on isolation work is reasonable and fits the slight hypertrophy benefit closer to failure (Robinson 2024; Grgic 2022 trained subgroup). The cost-benefit of going near failure is better on low-skill machine and cable exercises.
- Because of the ~1-rep underprediction and the worse accuracy above 12 reps (Halperin 2022), the "RIR 1" target on 12–20-rep sets (cable lateral raise, seated calf raise, cable hip abduction) likely lands nearer true RIR 2–3. A practical fix is periodic calibration: take the last set of a safe isolation exercise (cable lateral raise, leg extension, pushdown, leg curl) to true failure every 2–4 weeks to re-anchor estimates. Do not do this on RDL, squat or barbell press.
- The app's progression rule ("all sets at the top of range at target RIR") depends on RIR self-reports. A systematic ~1-rep underestimate makes the rule conservative: the user will tend to progress load later than needed. That is safe, but it may slow progression slightly.

### Gaps
- Robinson 2024's numeric slope (e.g., SMD change per RIR) was not in the abstract I retrieved, so I am not quoting a number. The secondary blog claim that the slope "tapered off past 2 RIR" is not verified against the paper.
- No trial directly compares RIR 1 vs. RIR 2 vs. RIR 3 on upper-body isolation exercises in trained lifters (Refalo 2024 used quadriceps only).

## Q2. Load / rep ranges: do 6–20 reps produce similar hypertrophy when taken close to failure?

### Takeaway
Yes. Strong meta-analytic evidence shows hypertrophy is similar across a wide load spectrum (roughly 5 to 30+ reps) when sets are taken to or near failure. Heavier loads (≤8RM) mainly add strength. The program's mix (6–8 on heavy compounds, 8–15 on secondary work, 12–20 on isolation) fits the evidence and needs no change for hypertrophy.

### Cited Findings
- Schoenfeld, Grgic, Ogborn & Krieger 2017 (Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research 31(12):3508–3523; 21 studies, all sets to momentary failure, low load ≤60% 1RM vs. high load >60% 1RM): hypertrophy changes were similar between conditions, while 1RM strength gains favoured high load. Their conclusion: "muscle hypertrophy can be equally achieved across a spectrum of loading ranges." — [PubMed 28834797](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28834797/)
- Lopez et al. 2021 (Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise; network meta-analysis, 28 studies, 747 adults, sets to volitional failure; low >15RM, moderate 9–15RM, high ≤8RM): no differences in hypertrophy between loads overall (P = 0.113–0.469) or in high-quality subgroups (P = 0.871–0.995). Strength gains were superior for high and moderate loads vs. low (SMD 0.60–0.63 and 0.34–0.35). High vs. moderate was non-significant (SMD 0.26–0.28, P = 0.068). Among participants with a training background, more RT sessions gave superior gains. A corrigendum exists. — [PubMed 33433148](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33433148/); [UWA repository](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/publications/resistance-training-load-effects-on-muscle-hypertrophy-and-streng/)
- Schoenfeld, Grgic, Van Every & Plotkin 2021 (Sports 9(2):32, narrative review, "A Re-Examination of the Repetition Continuum"): proposes a new paradigm in which "muscular adaptations can be obtained, and in some cases optimized, across a wide spectrum of loading zones." This rejects the idea that moderate loads are uniquely hypertrophic. — [PMC7927075](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7927075/)
- Plotkin et al. 2022 (PeerJ) summarise this literature as similar hypertrophy "between five and 30 or more repetitions" when sets are equated and performed with high effort. — [PMC9528903](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9528903/)

### Inferences
- 6–8 reps on the heaviest compounds gives a strength benefit (Lopez 2021; Schoenfeld 2017) that may support long-term load progression. For hypertrophy alone, 8–12 would be equally effective and lower in joint stress. Either choice is defensible.
- The 12–20 range on lateral raises and calves is fine for hypertrophy, but RIR is hardest to judge in this range (see Q1). Keep the upper end at ≤20 rather than higher.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence on whether a given muscle (e.g., side delts) responds better to a particular range. The meta-analyses pool mostly limb muscles (quadriceps, elbow flexors and extensors).

## Q3. Rest intervals: are 45–180 s appropriate, and is 60–75 s fine on isolation?

### Takeaway
Rests of at least 90 s on compounds are well supported. The best current meta-analysis finds a small hypertrophy benefit for resting more than 60 s, likely through better volume load, and no further benefit beyond about 90 s. The program's 60-s rests (cable lateral raise, seated calf raise, TKE, hip abduction, abs, carries) are at the low edge. Raising them to about 75–90 s is a low-cost, evidence-consistent tweak. Rows at 90 s and presses at 120–180 s are fine.

### Cited Findings
- Singer, Wolf et al. 2024 (Frontiers in Sports and Active Living 6:1429789; Bayesian meta-analysis, 9 studies, 19 measurements): effect sizes overlapped heavily across rest categories (short 0.48 vs. longer 0.56; four-category analysis: short 0.47, intermediate 0.65, long 0.55, very long 0.50). Controlled pairwise estimates slightly favoured longer rest for the arm (0.13, CrI −0.27 to 0.51) and thigh (0.17, CrI −0.13 to 0.43). Their conclusion: "a small hypertrophic benefit to employing inter-set rest interval durations >60 s, perhaps mediated by reductions in volume load," but "did not detect appreciable differences… when resting >90 s." Going to failure or stopping short did not change the rest–hypertrophy interaction. — [PubMed 39205815](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/); [PMC11349676](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11349676/)
- Schoenfeld et al. 2016 (Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research 30(7):1805–1812; 21 resistance-trained men; 8 weeks; 3 total-body sessions/week; 3 × 8–12RM of 7 exercises; 1-min vs. 3-min rest): 3-min rest produced greater 1RM squat and bench gains and greater anterior-thigh muscle thickness, with a trend for triceps (p = 0.06). — [PubMed 26605807](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26605807/)

### Inferences
- Heavy compounds at 150–180 s and DB presses at 120 s are consistent with Schoenfeld 2016 and Singer 2024. No change needed.
- Isolation at 60 s: Singer 2024 suggests the cutoff for a small benefit lies just above 60 s. Raising lateral raises, calf raises and hip abduction to about 75–90 s costs roughly 1–2 minutes per exercise. Pushdowns and curls at 75 s are acceptable.
- The 45-s rest on band external rotation (warm-up/prehab, no RIR) is irrelevant to hypertrophy and fine as is.
- Seated cable row at 90 s right after 6 sets of pulldowns is acceptable, given the plateau beyond about 90 s.

### Gaps
- The rest-interval evidence base is small (9 studies). Most studies were not on single-joint upper-body isolation such as lateral raises, so applying them to isolation exercises is extrapolation.
- The program file lists rest as "-s" for the first exercise of each superset and gives no rest between paired exercises. The actual time between sets of the *same* exercise is therefore unknown.

## Q4. Supersets / paired sets: effects on volume load and hypertrophy

### Takeaway
Agonist–antagonist and non-competing (different muscle) supersets keep volume load and long-term hypertrophy similar to traditional sets while saving roughly a third of session time. Supersets of the same muscle or similar movements *reduce* volume load. Most of the user's pairings are antagonist or non-competing, which is fine. The Mon Face Pull + Barbell Shrug pair is the weakest: both load the trapezius and the grip. The file also lists Barbell Shrugs twice (3 superset sets plus 4 straight sets).

### Cited Findings
- Zhang et al. 2025 (systematic review and meta-analysis, "Superset Versus Traditional Resistance Training Prescriptions…", PubMed 39903375; Weakley co-author), comparing supersets with traditional sets:
  - Total reps (SMD = −0.03, p = 0.92) and volume load (SMD = 0.05, p = 0.86) were similar.
  - Training efficiency was higher (SMD = 1.74, p = 0.01), with session time cut by about 37%.
  - Blood lactate, energy cost and RPE were higher (RPE SMD = 0.77).
  - Perceived recovery (SMD = 0.32, p = 0.33) and CK were similar.
  - Long-term hypertrophy was similar (SMD = −0.05, p = 0.87), as were strength (SMD = 0.10) and strength endurance.
  - Similar-biomechanics supersets reduced volume load (SMD = −1.08, p < 0.01).
  - Agonist–antagonist supersets did not differ significantly in volume load (SMD = 0.53, p = 0.09) but allowed more reps (SMD = 0.68, p = 0.01).
  - Prediction intervals were wide: volume load 95% PI −1.71 to 1.81.
  - Sources: [PMC12011898](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12011898/); [PubMed 39903375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39903375/); [Leeds Beckett eprint](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/11908/1/SupersetVersusTraditionalResistanceTrainingPrescriptionsASystematicReviewAndMetaanalysisExploringAcuteAndChronicPV-WEAKLEY.pdf)
- Weakley et al. 2017 (European Journal of Applied Physiology 117:1877–1889; 14 males, randomised crossover; traditional vs. superset vs. tri-set): session duration ranked traditional > superset > tri-set. Supersets and tri-sets raised lactate. CK was increased at 24 h for supersets ("very likely") and tri-sets ("likely"). The authors note supersets and tri-sets "may require additional recovery post-training." — [PMC5556132](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5556132/)
- Paz, Robbins et al. 2017 (Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research 31(10):2777–84) studied volume load and neuromuscular fatigue in agonist–antagonist paired sets vs. traditional sets. It is cited in the Zhang 2025 meta as part of the evidence that antagonist pairing maintains or raises volume. — [Leeds Beckett eprint reference list](https://eprints.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/id/eprint/11908/1/SupersetVersusTraditionalResistanceTrainingPrescriptionsASystematicReviewAndMetaanalysisExploringAcuteAndChronicPV-WEAKLEY.pdf)
- 2025 NSCA review (Strength and Conditioning Journal, "The Acute and Chronic Effects of Superset Resistance Training"): "chronic data generally indicate that supersets and TRAD lead to similar adaptations for endurance, hypertrophy, power, and strength." For less-trained clients it suggests lower set volume, longer rests within and between supersets, and not going to failure. — [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/The-Acute-and-Chronic-Effects-of-Superset.pdf)
- Superset pairings in the program file: Mon C1/C2 Face Pulls + Barbell Shrugs; Wed D1/D2 Single-Leg Extension + Seated/Lying Leg Curl; Wed E1/E2 Standing Calf Raise + Farmer's Carry; Fri A1/A2 Hammer Curl + Triceps Pushdown; Sat F1/F2 Pallof Press + Wrist/Reverse Curl. — program.txt (user file)

### Inferences
- Leg Extension + Leg Curl (quads/hamstrings) and Hammer Curl + Pushdown (elbow flexors/extensors) are classic agonist–antagonist pairs. Evidence says volume load is maintained or slightly increased. Keep them.
- Calf Raise + Farmer's Carry and Pallof + Wrist/Reverse Curl are non-competing pairs, which is fine. One caveat: the carry loads the calves and grip somewhat, so put the calf raise first, as the file already does.
- Face Pull + Barbell Shrug is neither antagonist nor fully separate. Both involve the trapezius (middle/lower for face pulls, upper for shrugs) and grip. The "similar-biomechanics" finding (SMD −1.08 volume load) may partly apply. Two options:
  - (a) Pair face pulls with an unrelated movement, e.g., a pressing or arm exercise.
  - (b) Keep the pair but allow about 60–90 s between the two exercises.
- The file lists "Face Pulls + Barbell Shrugs (superset) 3 × 12–15 + 10–12" and then a separate "Barbell Shrugs 4 × 10–12" line as C2. That is either a labelling duplicate or 7 shrug sets per session. The app's volume counting for traps should be checked.
- Supersets raise RPE and lactate (Zhang 2025; Weakley 2017). That matters for the app's psych/fatigue score: supersetted days may read as "harder" without meaning more hypertrophic stress.

### Gaps
- The Zhang 2025 journal name was not confirmed in the retrieved snippets (PMC/PubMed IDs are given). The 2025 Science & Sports trial "Less time, same gains" was found, but I could not retrieve its results. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0765159725001406)
- Very few long-term trials test supersets with sets taken to RIR 1–2 in trained lifters. Most chronic hypertrophy data come from a small number of studies.

## Q5. Progression model (double progression) and deloads

### Takeaway
Double progression is well supported in principle. Plotkin et al. 2022 showed that adding reps at a fixed load and adding load at fixed reps produce similar hypertrophy. The weak point is the fixed absolute load jumps on light dumbbell and cable lifts, which are large relative to the weights used. On deloads, the best RCT (Coleman et al. 2024) found that one week off did not impair hypertrophy but slightly reduced lower-body strength gains. Expert consensus (Bell et al. 2023) defines deloads as fatigue-management tools and recommends no fixed schedule. The app's composite score is consistent with that idea but has not itself been validated.

### Cited Findings
- Plotkin et al. 2022 (PeerJ 10:e14142; 43 trained participants, ≥1 year of lower-body training; 8 weeks; 4 sets of 4 lower-body exercises twice a week; LOAD = add weight at fixed reps vs. REPS = add reps at fixed load):
  - Rectus femoris growth modestly favoured REPS (sum of sites 2.8 mm, CI90% −0.5 to 5.8).
  - Squat 1RM slightly favoured LOAD (2.0 kg, CI90% −2.4 to 7.8).
  - Other muscle-thickness differences were <1 mm.
  - Conclusion: "Both progressions of repetitions and load appear to be viable strategies."
  - Sources: [PubMed 36199287](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36199287/); [PMC9528903](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9528903/)
- Coleman et al. 2024 (PeerJ 12:e16777; 39 trained participants: 29 men, 10 women; 9-week high-volume program; DELOAD = 1 week of complete cessation at the midpoint vs. continuous TRAD):
  - There were "no appreciable differences" in lower-body muscle size, local endurance or power.
  - TRAD showed greater gains in isometric and dynamic lower-body strength.
  - TRAD showed "slight psychological benefits" on a readiness-to-train questionnaire.
  - Upper-body training was unsupervised.
  - Sources: [PubMed 38274324](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38274324/); [PMC10809978](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10809978/)
- Bell, Strafford, Coleman, Androulakis Korakakis & Nolan 2023 (Sports Medicine – Open 9:87; international Delphi; 34 → 29 → 21 expert coaches over 3 rounds; 138 statements):
  - Consensus definition: "Deloading is a period of reduced training stress designed to mitigate physiological and psychological fatigue, promote recovery, and enhance preparedness for subsequent training."
  - This is expert opinion, not trial evidence.
  - Sources: [PubMed 37730925](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37730925/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-023-00633-0)
- 2026 within-subject trial in untrained young men (Scientific Reports, "Effects of deload periods in resistance training on muscle hypertrophy and strength endurance…"):
  - Deload and continuous conditions produced comparable hypertrophy over 8 weeks; all 95% CIs of the differences included zero (e.g., mid-quadriceps 50%: −1.08 mm, CI −2.75 to 0.59).
  - The deload condition did about 18% fewer sets (46 vs. 56).
  - The authors also note that 7–10 days of detraining can reduce muscle thickness and fibre CSA in some studies.
  - Source: [Nature Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-40612-5)
- Halperin 2022: RIR predictions have a mean error of about 1 rep and between-person SD of 1.45 reps (see Q1). — [PubMed 34542869](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34542869/)

### Inferences
- **Double progression is sound.** Plotkin 2022 supports adding reps as a valid overload stimulus, so "reps first, then load" fits the evidence.
- **Load jumps are too coarse for light lifts** (inference; no trial tests jump size). For a 73-kg recreational lifter:
  - A +2 kg dumbbell jump on, e.g., 10–14 kg dumbbells is a 14–20% increase. In a 10–12 range (alternating DB curl, flat/decline DB press) that will often drop reps below the floor.
  - A +1 kg jump on a ~5–8 kg cable lateral raise is 12–20%.
  - Fixes: microplates or 0.5–1 kg cable increments where available; widen narrow ranges (e.g., 10–12 → 8–12 or 10–15); or allow sets *within* the range after a jump instead of forcing all sets to the bottom.
  - The heavy-compound 6–8 range (incline barbell press, RDL, weighted pull-ups) is only 2 reps wide. With +2.5 kg jumps that is usually workable on a barbell, but for weighted pull-ups at 73 kg bodyweight +2.5 kg is a meaningful relative jump. Allowing +1.25 kg steps would be smoother.
- **The "all sets at top of range at target RIR" trigger** is conservative because of the ~1-rep RIR underprediction (Halperin 2022). This slows progression but is not harmful. A rule such as "first set hits top of range at ≥ target RIR, and the remaining sets are within 1–2 reps" would progress faster. That rule is practitioner judgement, not trial-validated.
- **The deload score is plausible but unvalidated.** It fits the Bell 2023 definition (fatigue mitigation, readiness).
  - The 1RM-decline input will be noisy if 1RM is *estimated* from RIR-based sets, since RIR error is about 1 rep (Halperin 2022). Use a multi-session trend or average rather than a single session.
  - Coleman 2024 suggests a deload is not needed to protect hypertrophy and may cost a little strength if it is a full week off. A reduced-volume deload (e.g., about half the sets at RIR 3–4) rather than complete cessation is the more conservative choice. This is inference: Coleman tested complete cessation, not reduced volume.
  - Because the program's RIR 1–2 already limits fatigue (Refalo 2024: failure produced more velocity and rep loss), deloads can likely be infrequent and triggered by need.
- The superset days and RIR-1 isolation work raise perceived exertion (Zhang 2025; Refalo EJSC). The psych component of the score may fire on those days without real fatigue. Smoothing the psych component over a week or more would reduce false triggers. This is inference.

### Gaps
- No RCT compares double progression with other progression models (fixed periodization, RIR-autoregulated load, percentage-based) for hypertrophy in recreational lifters. Plotkin 2022 is the closest, covering lower body only over 8 weeks.
- No study validates a composite deload trigger (1RM decline + psych + volume). The thresholds the app uses cannot be checked against evidence.
- I did not retrieve the specific Bell 2023 consensus statements on deload *frequency* (e.g., "every 4–8 weeks") or on content (volume vs. intensity reduction), only the definition and method. A report writer should not quote a frequency from this source without checking the full text ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40798-023-00633-0)).
- Evidence on deloads in *trained* lifters for upper-body hypertrophy is thin; Coleman 2024's upper-body training was unsupervised.
- I did not retrieve an ACSM or NSCA position stand on these variables. The 2009 ACSM progression stand predates most of the evidence above.
