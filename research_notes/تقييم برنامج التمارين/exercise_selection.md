# Exercise Selection, Muscle Length/ROM, Split Structure and Joint Protection: Evaluation of the User's 4-Day Upper/Lower Program

Scope note: user is a recreational lifter, ~73 kg, goal muscle gain, with an apparent knee concern (TKE "for knee protection", box/goblet squat instead of back squat) and shoulder prehab (band external rotation). Nothing below is medical advice. A knee or shoulder that hurts should be assessed by a clinician (physio or sports-medicine doctor).

Program facts used below (from program.txt). These are direct weekly sets counted from the file, not research findings:
- Chest pressing: Incline Barbell 4 + Flat DB 3 + Decline DB 3 = 10 direct, plus Landmine/Neutral DB OHP 4 (labelled upper chest/front delt). No fly or other long-length chest isolation.
- Vertical pull: Neutral-Grip Pulldown 6 + Weighted Pull-ups 4 = 10. Horizontal pull: Seated Cable Row 3 + Chest-Supported Row 6 = 9.
- Side delts: Cable Lateral Raise 4 + 4 = 8. Rear delts: Face Pulls 3 + 2, Rear Delt Fly 3 = 8 direct, plus 19 rowing/pulling sets.
- Traps: Barbell Shrugs 4, Farmer's Carry 3. The Monday block lists "Face Pulls + Barbell Shrugs (superset) 3 sets" and then "Barbell Shrugs 4 sets" as C2, so it is unclear whether shrugs are 3 or 4 sets, or 7. This should be clarified in the program.
- Triceps direct: Rope Pushdown 3 + Triceps Pushdown 2 = 5, all done with the arm at the side. No overhead or other long-length triceps work.
- Biceps direct: Alternating DB Curl 2 + Hammer Curl 2 = 4. No long-length biceps work (incline or behind-the-body cable curl).
- Quads: Box/Goblet Squat 3 + Leg Press (high foot) 3 + Single-Leg Extension 3 + Hack Squat/Leg Press 3 = 12, plus TKE 3 + 3 (low load).
- Hamstrings: "Seated or Lying" Leg Curl 3 + 3 = 6 knee-flexion sets, plus RDL 3 (hip extension).
- Glutes: Hip Thrust 1 set, Cable Hip Abduction 2, plus squat/leg press/RDL indirectly. Adductors: no direct work.
- Calves: Standing 3 + Seated 3 = 6. Abs 4. Forearms 2, plus carries, plus an optional Sunday 2.
- Split: Upper A (Mon), Lower A (Wed), Upper B (Fri), Lower B (Sat), so each muscle is trained twice a week.

## Q1. Training at long muscle lengths / stretch-mediated hypertrophy (Maeo 2021, Maeo 2023, Pedrosa 2022, Kassiano 2023, Wolf 2023)

### Takeaway
There is consistent, moderate-quality evidence, mostly from short within-subject trials on isolation exercises, that training a muscle at longer lengths grows it as much as or more than training it at shorter lengths. This is strongest for the seated leg curl (over lying), overhead triceps extension (over pushdown) and long-length calf work. For this program it means: pick the seated leg curl rather than leaving it as "seated or lying", add overhead triceps extensions, and bias some biceps and calf work toward the stretched position. Full ROM remains the default. Lengthened partials are an optional tool, not a requirement.

### Cited Findings
- **Maeo et al. 2021 (Med Sci Sports Exerc), seated vs prone leg curl, 12 weeks, MRI.** Whole-hamstring volume rose more with seated than prone curls (+14% vs +9%). Each biarticular head grew more (+8–24% vs +4–19%). The monoarticular short head of biceps femoris did not differ (+10% vs +9%). Biceps femoris long head grew about 2.2-fold more with seated curls. The authors recommend the seated rather than prone leg curl when the aim is hamstring size, and say seated curls "may reduce the need for substantial volumes of other hamstring exercises". Both versions protected similarly against later muscle damage. — [PubMed 33009197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7969179/)
- **Maeo et al. (Eur J Sport Sci; epub 2022, print 2023), overhead vs neutral-arm cable elbow extension, 12 weeks, 21 untrained subjects, MRI, 5×10 twice a week, within-subject design.** Whole triceps grew about 1.4-fold more with overhead extensions (+19.9% vs +13.9%, d = 0.54, P < 0.001). Long head: +28.5% vs +19.6%. The single-joint lateral and medial heads also grew more: +14.6% vs +10.5% (P = 0.002). This happened even though the overhead arm lifted lower absolute loads. The authors' conclusion: "Cable elbow extension training should be performed in the overhead rather than neutral arm position if one aims to maximise muscle hypertrophy of the triceps brachii." — [PubMed 35819335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/); details from [Stronger by Science review](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/research-spotlight-triceps/)
- **Conflicting earlier evidence.** Stasinaki et al. 2018 (J Funct Morphol Kinesiol, ultrasound) found similar long-head growth with overhead extensions and pushdowns. Stronger by Science suggests three possible reasons: lower measurement quality, the fixed exercise order, and an overhead ROM (150°–70° elbow flexion) that may over-stretch the long head. That last point is commentary, not data. — [Stronger by Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/research-spotlight-triceps/)
- **Pedrosa et al. 2022 (Eur J Sport Sci 22(8):1250–1260), leg extension.** Compared full ROM, initial (lengthened) partials, final (shortened) partials and a varied ROM. Conclusion: "Partial range of motion training elicits favorable improvements in muscular adaptations when carried out at long muscle lengths". Regional quadriceps (vastus lateralis and rectus femoris) hypertrophy was favoured with long-length training. — [PubMed 33977835](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33977835/); [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1080/17461391.2021.1927199)
- **Kassiano et al. 2023 (J Strength Cond Res 37(9):1746–1753), calf raises in young women, 8 weeks.** Partial ROM in the lengthened position produced more gastrocnemius growth. The authors conclude that "calf training performed at longer muscle lengths may optimize gastrocnemius muscle hypertrophy". Sources disagree on the detail. One citing paper reports the benefit for the medial head with no difference in the lateral head ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/365127382_Greater_gastrocnemius_muscle_hypertrophy_after_partial_range_of_motion_training_performed_at_long_muscle_lengths)). A 2025 systematic review describes greater growth in both heads ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666337625000332)). The garbled abstract text suggests medial-head figures of about +15.2% (lengthened) vs +6.7% (full) vs +3.4% (shortened). Verify these against the full text before quoting them. — [PubMed 37015016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37015016/)
- **Wolf et al. 2023 (Int J Strength Cond), partial vs full ROM meta-analysis.** A subgroup analysis "suggested there may be a muscle hypertrophy benefit to partial ROM training at long muscle lengths compared to using a full ROM" (−0.28; 95% CI −0.81, 0.16). The confidence interval crosses zero, so this is not conclusive. — [IUSCA Journal](https://journal.iusca.org/index.php/Journal/article/view/182)
- **Systematic review, 2025 (ScienceDirect, journal pii S2666337625000332), longer- vs shorter-muscle-length RT.** "LML-RT consistently leads to greater muscle hypertrophy than SML-RT". The finding of greater fascicle length (longitudinal growth) "remains equivocal". — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666337625000332)
- **Trained lifters, lengthened partials vs full ROM (within-participant, upper limbs, 30 resistance-trained participants).** Titled "Lengthened partial repetitions elicit similar muscular adaptations as full ROM", i.e. equivalence rather than superiority in trained people. — [PubMed 39959841](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39959841/)
- **Eur J Sport Sci 2025, 8 weeks, trained individuals, elbow flexors.** Initial-phase partials (0°–70°) vs full ROM (0°–140°). Results were similar at 50% of humeral length. There was a trivial-to-small advantage for partials at 70% (SMD = 0.10, Bayes factor 4.87), interpreted as "modest benefits for regional hypertrophy". — [PMC12621570](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621570/)
- **Plotkin et al. 2023.** In a compound lift the lengthened-position advantage may not carry over. Squat and hip thrust grew the glutes similarly despite their different loading positions. The authors suggest "the context in which the muscle is experiencing lengthened loading critically determines subsequent adaptations". — [Frontiers in Physiology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full)

### Inferences
- **Leg curl (Wed and Sat):** change "Seated or Lying" to Seated Leg Curl as the default, on both days or at least one. This is the single best-supported swap in the program (Maeo 2021, MRI, about 1.5× whole-hamstring growth).
- **Triceps:** replace the Friday "Triceps Pushdown" (2 sets), or the Monday rope pushdown, with an overhead cable or DB triceps extension. Keep some pushdowns for variety and elbow comfort if wanted. Avoid going so deep that elbow flexion goes far past about 90–120° overhead, given the over-stretch concern raised by Stronger by Science.
- **Biceps:** no RCT directly testing incline DB curls or behind-the-body cable curls was found. The extrapolation rests on the elbow-flexor partial-ROM data (PMC12621570) and the general long-length literature. Swapping the alternating DB curl for an incline DB curl or a Bayesian/behind-body cable curl is a reasonable, low-risk choice, not a proven one.
- **Calves:** keep both. Emphasise a full stretch and pause at the bottom of the standing raise, and optionally add lengthened partials at the end of sets (Kassiano 2023). The seated calf raise mainly trains soleus; the gastrocnemius data do not directly apply to it.
- **Leg extension:** a full-ROM or lengthened-biased single-leg extension is supported for quad (rectus femoris) growth (Pedrosa 2022). Whether deep knee flexion under load is comfortable for this user's knee is a separate question; see Q5.
- **Lengthened partials** in general are an option for trained lifters. They are not clearly superior to full ROM.

### Gaps
- Pedrosa 2023 (a follow-up on lengthened partials) could not be confirmed with exact citation or numbers in this search. Pedrosa 2022's numbers by muscle region were not retrieved.
- No RCT found on incline curls specifically, on long-length chest (fly) hypertrophy, or on lateral-raise variations (cable vs DB, lengthened) for side-delt growth.
- The Wolf 2023 point estimate's sign convention was taken from the abstract wording. The full-text effect direction was not verified.

## Q2. Region-specific hypertrophy: incline vs flat press, "upper/lower chest", lats vs mid-back, hip thrust vs squat, RDL, hamstring pairing, adductors

### Takeaway
There is limited evidence that incline pressing biases growth of the upper (clavicular) pec. There is no direct evidence that decline pressing builds a "lower chest" better than flat pressing. Hip thrust and squat grow the glutes similarly, but squats also grow the quads and adductors. Deep squats grow the adductors and glutes more than half squats. Pairing a hip-extension hamstring exercise with a seated knee-flexion exercise is well supported. The program's "lat width vs mid-back" split is a convention, not a research finding.

### Cited Findings
- **Chaves et al. 2020 (Int J Exerc Sci), untrained young men; incline only vs flat only vs combined.** Growth at the 2nd intercostal space (clavicular/upper pec) was greatest in the incline group compared with the horizontal group (mean difference 0.62 cm, 95% CI 0.23–1.0, p = 0.003) and the combination group (0.50 cm, p = 0.008). There were no group differences at the 3rd or 5th intercostal spaces (p = 0.095 and p = 0.227). — [PMC7449336](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7449336/). Stronger by Science calls the incline group's >62% upper-pec increase "an unheard of increase that stands out as an outlier." — [Stronger by Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/regional-hypertrophy/)
- **Acute study, trained participants (J Electromyogr Kinesiol 2022, panoramic ultrasound).** Incline bench produced more clavicular than sternocostal excitation and cross-sectional-area swelling; flat bench showed the opposite pattern. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1050641122000955). Stronger by Science notes that the only between-exercise difference was greater sternal-head swelling after flat bench, and it warns that acute swelling does not reliably predict long-term growth. — [Stronger by Science](https://www.strongerbyscience.com/regional-hypertrophy/)
- I could not verify a "Chaves 2024" incline-vs-flat study. The available Chaves trial is from 2020 (above).
- **Plotkin et al. 2023 (Front Physiol 14:1279170), 9 weeks, set-volume-equated back squat vs barbell hip thrust in untrained participants (18 HT, 16 SQ).** Upper, mid and lower glute growth was similar between groups, with estimates modestly but not clearly favouring hip thrust. Squats produced more quadriceps growth (3.6 cm², CI 0.7–6.4) and more adductor growth (2.5 cm², CI 1.2–3.9). Neither group grew the hamstrings meaningfully. Hip thrust EMG was higher but "did not consistently predict gluteal hypertrophy." The authors call the hip thrust "more gluteus maximus-specific". The paper received a correction in 2026; the correction's content was not reviewed. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full); [PubMed 37877099](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37877099/)
- **Kubo et al. 2019 (Eur J Appl Physiol), full squat (about 140° knee flexion) vs half squat (about 90°), 10 weeks, MRI.** Adductor volume rose 6.2% vs 2.7% and gluteus maximus 6.7% vs 2.2%, both significantly greater with full squats (p = 0.026 and p = 0.008). Hamstrings did not change in either group. — [PubMed 31230110](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31230110/)
- **Maeo 2021 (from Q1).** The seated leg curl grew all three biarticular hamstrings, which the authors say is "unachievable through either the Nordic Hamstring or the hip extension alone". They note that various exercises may be needed to train the whole hamstring comprehensively. — [PMC7969179](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7969179/)
- **Kassiano et al. 2022 (J Strength Cond Res), systematic review.** "Varying exercise selection can influence muscle hypertrophy and strength gains." — [PubMed 35438660](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35438660/)

### Inferences
- **Chest:** Incline Barbell (Mon) plus Landmine/Neutral OHP (Fri) gives reasonable upper-pec emphasis. "Decline DB Press — lower chest" is not supported as a necessary regional exercise. Flat pressing already biases the sternal head. If the user wants a stretch-biased chest exercise (long-length logic extrapolated from Q1, not directly tested for pecs), Decline DB Press could be swapped for a cable or DB fly, or a deep-stretch machine press.
- **Glutes:** a single hip-thrust set to near failure is low on its own. But the squat, leg press and RDL already train the glutes (Plotkin 2023; Kubo 2019), so direct glute volume is probably adequate. The main limit is that the box squat depth decides how much glute and adductor stimulus the squat provides (Kubo 2019). If the knee tolerates it, a box set at or below parallel is preferable.
- **Adductors:** there is no direct adductor exercise. The adductor magnus is a major hip extensor, and the program's squats are knee-friendly but possibly shallow. Options: add 2–3 sets of a hip-adduction machine or a Copenhagen-style adduction, or rely on deeper squats, RDLs and the leg press (an indirect stimulus per Plotkin 2023 and Kubo 2019). Adding a direct exercise is a judgment call, not research-mandated.
- **Hamstrings:** RDL (hip extension, Sat) plus seated leg curl (knee flexion, twice a week) is the evidence-aligned pairing. The program already has this structure; it just needs "seated" specified.
- **Back:** splitting the pulldown and pull-up into "lat width" and the rows into "mid-back" is coaching convention. Both pull types train the lats and mid-back. The current 10 vertical and 9 horizontal sets are balanced.

### Gaps
- No longitudinal study found that compares decline vs flat pressing for lower-pec hypertrophy.
- No RCT found comparing vertical vs horizontal pulls for regional lat or mid-back hypertrophy.
- No hypertrophy trial found for direct adductor isolation in trained lifters.
- Plotkin 2023 studied untrained people, and the 2026 correction was not reviewed.

## Q3. Gaps and redundancies in this specific program

### Takeaway
The main gaps are: no long-length triceps work, a "seated or lying" curl choice that should be seated, low direct biceps volume (4 sets), and no direct adductor work. The rear-delt and trap work looks redundant: 8 direct rear-delt sets on top of 19 rowing and pulling sets, an ambiguous shrug entry, and farmer's carries. Quads are trained twice a week with about 12 working sets, which is in line with the program's own MEV–MAV range.

### Cited Findings
- **Pelland et al. 2025 (Sports Medicine; DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w), 67 studies, 2,058 participants.** The "fractional" method, which counts indirect sets as 0.5 sets, "best predicted muscle hypertrophy and strength gains". Hypertrophy increases with weekly set volume (posterior probability 100% that the slope exceeds zero), with diminishing returns that are weaker for hypertrophy than for strength. — [PubMed 41343037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/); [PDF](https://www.fisiologiadelejercicio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/The-Resistance-Training-Dose-Response.pdf)
- **Maeo 2022/2023 (triceps, see Q1).** Overhead extension grew even the single-joint triceps heads more than pushdowns. — [PubMed 35819335](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/)
- **Maeo 2021 (hamstrings, see Q1).** — [PubMed 33009197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33009197/)

### Inferences
These are applied judgments built on the counts in the scope section and the fractional method.
- **Triceps:** 5 direct sets + 0.5 × about 14 pressing sets ≈ 12 fractional sets. Volume is adequate. The problem is exercise choice: there is no overhead extension. Swap one pushdown slot for an overhead cable extension.
- **Biceps:** 4 direct sets + 0.5 × 19 pulling sets ≈ 13.5 fractional sets. Probably adequate for growth. If arms are a priority, add 2 sets of an incline or behind-body cable curl.
- **Rear delts and traps:** Face Pulls (3 + 2) + Rear Delt Fly 3 = 8 direct rear-delt sets, while 19 rowing and pulling sets already load the rear delts and mid traps. The Friday Face Pulls (2 sets) are the most redundant item and could be dropped or moved. The shrug entry is duplicated or ambiguous (3 sets in the C1 superset line, plus 4 sets as C2). Combined with farmer's carries, upper-trap work is generous. Removing the duplication saves time.
- **Quads:** about 12 direct sets across two days. TKE (6 sets) is low-load and is better counted as warm-up than as hypertrophy volume (see Q5). There is no deficiency.
- **Hamstrings:** 6 curl sets + 3 RDL sets ≈ 9. Adequate, provided seated curls are used.
- **Glutes:** 1 hip thrust + 2 abduction, plus about 9 indirect sets. Probably adequate (see Q2).
- **Adductors:** a gap (see Q2).
- **Calves:** 6 sets. At the program's own MEV; could be raised if calves are a priority.
- **Chest:** the decline press is the most dispensable chest slot (see Q2).
- **Side delts:** 8 sets of cable lateral raises. Adequate, with no redundancy.
- **Pulldown, 6 consecutive sets on Monday:** high for a single exercise in one session. Splitting it into two exercises, for example 3 pulldown + 3 single-arm pulldown or cable pullover, is a variation option (Kassiano 2022 review), not a requirement.

### Gaps
- No direct research on the ideal number of rear-delt sets when rowing volume is high. The redundancy call is inference based on fractional set counting.

## Q4. Upper/lower 4-day split vs full-body vs PPL when volume is equated

### Takeaway
When weekly volume is equated, split and full-body routines produce similar hypertrophy and strength (Ramos-Campo 2024). Training frequency on its own has, at most, a small effect on hypertrophy (Pelland 2025). The user's upper/lower split, which trains each muscle twice a week, is fully supported, and the choice of split should rest on preference and schedule.

### Cited Findings
- **Ramos-Campo et al. 2024 (J Strength Cond Res 38(7):1330–1340), split vs full-body systematic review and meta-analysis, 14 studies, 392 subjects.** Hypertrophy outcomes were similar: elbow extensors MD = 0.30 [−2.65, 3.24], p = 0.84; elbow flexors MD = 0.17, p = 0.91; vastus lateralis MD = −0.08, p = 0.93; lean mass p = 0.92. Strength outcomes were also similar: bench p = 0.34, lower limb p = 0.29. The authors conclude it provides "solid evidence that the use of Sp or FB routines ... does not significantly impact either strength gains or muscle hypertrophy when volume is equated. Consequently, individuals are free to confidently select a resistance training routine based on their personal preferences." — [PubMed 38595233](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38595233/); [ResearchGate abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379724552_Efficacy_of_Split_Versus_Full-Body_Resistance_Training_on_Strength_and_Muscle_Growth_A_Systematic_Review_With_Meta-Analysis)
- **Pelland et al. 2025 (Sports Medicine).** The effect of frequency on hypertrophy is "compatible with negligible effects" (posterior probability below 100%). For strength, the posterior probability was 100%: strength gains increase with frequency, with diminishing returns. — [PubMed 41343037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/)
- **Single trial in trained men, cited on ResearchGate.** A 10-week total-body vs split-routine study found greater vastus lateralis growth with the split and some isokinetic strength advantage with total-body. Individual trials therefore vary, but the pooled result is null. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379724552_Efficacy_of_Split_Versus_Full-Body_Resistance_Training_on_Strength_and_Muscle_Growth_A_Systematic_Review_With_Meta-Analysis)

### Inferences
- The 4-day upper/lower split with active-recovery days (swim, sauna) is well suited to a recreational lifter. There is no evidence-based reason to switch to full-body or a push/pull/legs split for hypertrophy.
- Because frequency matters little once volume is matched, sessions can be kept shorter by cutting the redundant sets identified in Q3. This should not affect growth.
- Upper days are long: Monday has 10 entries, 30+ sets and long rests (180 s on incline). Pelland 2025 is about weekly volume, so moving some sets to another day is acceptable.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis found that compares push/pull/legs specifically against upper/lower. The Ramos-Campo 2024 categories were split vs full-body. Most included trials ran 4–12 weeks.

## Q5. Joint protection: knee (TKE, box squat, leg extension, patellofemoral pain) and shoulder (external-rotation prehab, face pulls, landmine press)

### Takeaway
For patellofemoral-type knee pain, the best evidence (2019 JOSPT clinical practice guideline; 2024 BJSM best-practice guide) supports progressive knee-targeted exercise, both resisted squats and resisted knee extension, combined with posterolateral hip strengthening and education. The leg extension is a recommended knee-targeted exercise in these guidelines, not a contraindicated one. TKE has no specific trial support for prevention or hypertrophy that I could find. For the shoulder, I found no strong evidence that band external rotation, face pulls or the landmine press prevent injury in recreational lifters. Most of the prevention evidence comes from overhead and throwing athletes. This is not medical advice.

### Cited Findings
- **Willy et al. 2019, Patellofemoral Pain CPG (J Orthop Sports Phys Ther 49(9):CPG1–CPG95).** "Clinicians should include exercise therapy with combined hip- and knee-targeted exercises to reduce pain and improve patient-reported outcomes and functional performance in the short, medium, and long term." The guideline also states:
  - Hip-targeted exercise should target the posterolateral hip.
  - "Knee-targeted exercise therapy includes either weight-bearing (resisted squats) or non–weight-bearing (resisted knee extension) exercise."
  - Hip exercise may be preferred in the early stages.
  - Two meta-analyses found small short-term effects favouring hip-targeted over knee-targeted exercise for pain (SMD 0.36, 95% CI 0.13–0.59).
  - Leg presses, step-ups and squats are classed as knee-targeted exercises.
  — [JOSPT CPG](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2019.0302); [PubMed 31475628](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31475628/). Bottom line from the companion JOSPT patient page: "Exercise therapy that is appropriately progressed and focused on hip and knee strengthening is the best approach to managing patellofemoral pain." — [JOSPT 2019;49(9):631–632](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2019.0503)
- **Neal, Lack, Bartholomew, Morrissey 2024, best practice guide for PFP (Br J Sports Med 58(24):1486–1495).** Based on 65 high-quality RCTs (3,796 participants) plus interviews with patients and clinicians. Its recommendations:
  - "Knee-targeted±hip-targeted exercise therapy underpinned by education should be delivered."
  - Exercise should be "prescribed relative to specific severity and irritability, with a greater focus on hip exercises in people with poor tolerance to loaded knee flexion, with task/load/intensity/frequency modified as required."
  - It notes "Quadriceps weakness has an established prospective association with PFP development."
  - Supporting options: foot orthoses, taping, manual therapy, running retraining.
  — [BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/58/24/1486)
- **Earlier consensus statements:** the 2018 Gold Coast consensus (Collins et al., BJSM) and the 2016 Manchester consensus (Crossley et al., BJSM). Both support exercise therapy, particularly combined hip and knee exercises. — [PubMed 29925502](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29925502/); [2016 PDF](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/97316/REPUB_97316-OA.pdf)
- **Open-kinetic-chain knee extension after ACL reconstruction.** An IJSPT clinical commentary states that open-chain knee extension "can be used safely following ACLR and must be an integral part of the rehabilitation process" (opinion/commentary level). — [IJSPT](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/18983-considerations-with-open-kinetic-chain-knee-extension-exercise-following-acl-reconstruction)
- **Terminal Knee Extension:** my search found no RCT that specifically tests band TKE for knee-pain prevention or quad hypertrophy in healthy lifters. The results returned were TKA rehab studies of knee-extension velocity, which are not relevant to TKE. — [PMC5294937](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5294937/) (not TKE-specific)
- **Shoulder:** reviews of prevention in overhead and throwing athletes include external-rotation and scapular strengthening, and stress attention to external-rotation strength. — [PMC11545345](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11545345/); [J Exp Orthop (ESSKA)](https://esskajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1186/s40634-022-00493-9); [Phys Ther Sport systematic review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1466853X21001516). These are athlete populations, not recreational lifters.

### Inferences
These are applied judgments, not medical advice.
- **Knee: the program is broadly consistent with PFP guidance.** It includes knee-targeted work (box/goblet squat, leg press, hack squat, single-leg extension) and hip-targeted work (cable hip abduction, hip thrust, RDL).
  - Because the 2019 CPG gives weight to the posterolateral hip, the hip abduction (currently 2 sets, Sat only) could be made a fixed slot on both lower days.
- **Box squat and goblet squat** are sensible tolerance-friendly options. Guidance says to modify task and load to irritability, for example box height and depth, and to progress depth as tolerated. There is a trade-off: shallower squats give less glute and adductor stimulus (Kubo 2019).
- **Leg extension:** guidelines list resisted knee extension as a recommended knee-targeted exercise. The single-leg extension is therefore justified, and with progressive load it is more defensible than TKE for quad strength.
  - With knee pain, the ROM used in leg extensions can be limited to a pain-acceptable range and progressed over time.
  - Specific angle ranges were not verified in this search. That detail should come from a clinician.
- **TKE (6 sets a week):** low cost and harmless as a warm-up, but there is no evidence found that it "protects" the knee. Its hypertrophy contribution is likely minimal. It could be cut to 1–2 warm-up sets, freeing time for the hip abduction or adductor work.
- **Shoulder prehab:** band external rotation (2×15 per side) is cheap warm-up work with plausible rationale but weak direct evidence in lifters.
  - With rear-delt fly, rowing and face pulls also in the program, the shoulder already gets plenty of external-rotation and posterior work. Keeping band ER as a warm-up is fine. Calling it injury prevention overstates the evidence.
- **Landmine press:** I found no trials comparing it with overhead or bench pressing for shoulder pain. Using it as a "shoulder-friendly" press rests on clinical reasoning and user tolerance, not trial data.
- **Pain is the guide:** if the knee or shoulder hurts during training, see a physiotherapist, who can individualise exercise selection and load (Neal 2024; Willy 2019).

### Gaps
- No RCTs found for band TKE, face pulls or the landmine press as injury-prevention tools in recreational lifters.
- No data were retrieved on leg-extension angle ranges and patellofemoral joint stress.
- The user's actual knee diagnosis is unknown (PFP, tendinopathy, ligament, meniscus). The PFP guidance applies only if the problem is PFP-type anterior knee pain.
- Shoulder-prevention reviews concern overhead athletes; I found no equivalent evidence for gym lifters.
